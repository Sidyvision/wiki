## MODULE 1 — Identity and origin

**Critical distinction, to be held throughout.** Two entities share the name:

| | Kojima Productions (I) | Kojima Productions (II) |
|---|---|---|
| Period | c. 2005 – 2015 | Dec. 2015 – present |
| Status | Internal division of Konami | Independent company |
| Legal continuity | None with (II) | New entity |

Conflating the two is the most common error in the available commentary. This
study covers **(II)**, with (I) as prehistory.

- **Founded**: December 2015, Tokyo `to-source` (tier C).
- **Founder**: Hideo Kojima — the authorial profile is held in the `hermeneutique/` circuit and is
  deliberately not linked from here (see note on watertightness below). Symbolic capital at founding: roughly thirty years of
  authored work and a personal name already functioning as a mark.
- **Founding event / founding wound**: the 2015 rupture with Konami. Reported
  elements — removal of the director's name from the marketing of the final
  *Metal Gear Solid* title, dispersal of the team, cancellation of a horror
  project after a widely circulated playable teaser, and the director's absence
  from a major industry awards ceremony (tier C, contested in detail).
- **Starting capital**: undisclosed. A publishing agreement with a platform
  holder covered the first title's development `to-source` (tier C).
- **Market context at founding**: consolidation of large publishers around
  service-based and franchise products; contraction of the mid-size Japanese
  auteur studio. The vacancy filled was narrow and specific — a large-budget
  studio built around a single named director.

**Analysis.** The founding answers an **inner necessity**, not a market gap: the
studio exists because a body of work needed somewhere to continue. The founder's
history and the house's DNA are not merely related — they are the same object,
which is both the source of coherence and the central structural risk (Module 9).

**Signals.** *Strong*: founding by inner necessity — a predictor of long
coherence. *Weak*: a founding wound framed publicly as a break with an old
employer risks fixing the house's identity in opposition rather than in
proposition.

## MODULE 2 — Thesis and positioning

**Founding conviction, in one sentence**: a game can carry an author's thesis
the way a novel or a film carries one, and should be financed and marketed on
that basis.

**Space occupied**: large-budget authored work, produced slowly, on original
intellectual property.

**Space explicitly refused** (as declared; verification `to-source`): licensed
franchise work, and — for the first title — the safety of a proven genre. The
studio's first release deliberately invented its own category rather than
entering an existing one.

**Competitors**: not other studios, properly speaking. The competition is for
**publisher risk appetite**. The real peer set is the small group of
director-led studios operating at large budget worldwide.

**Evolution**: from single-publisher dependence toward a portfolio of
simultaneous projects with different partners, plus a declared expansion beyond
games into film, television and music `to-source` (tier C).

**Analysis.** The differentiation is **structural, not cosmetic**: it lies in the
production model (one director, original IP, long cycles), not in tone or visual
style. Whether the positioning survives the founder is the open question of this
study, and it is not answered by anything currently public.

**Signals.** *Strong*: documented capacity to refuse — the entire 2015 rupture
reads as a refusal. *Weak*: the positioning is **incarnated, not codified**; no
public evidence of a written charter, method or succession doctrine.

## MODULE 3 — Art direction and brand

- **Visual identity**: internal, under a long-standing art director. Continuity of hand from 1994 to
  present, across two distinct companies, is the single most remarkable brand
  fact of this case.
- **Emblem**: an extravehicular-suited figure bearing a lance and shield,
  attached to a declared motto on play as a defining human trait `to-source`.
  The emblem functions as a mascot, a merchandising line and a signature.
- **Name**: the founder's surname. Maximum legibility, maximum dependency.
- **Signature/anonymity**: the opposite of anonymity. The authorial credit is
  displayed on the work itself as a formal device.
- **Canon management**: complicated by the fact that the founder's best-known
  earlier work belongs to a former employer. The house therefore cannot
  administer its own back catalogue — an unusual and structurally important
  constraint.

**Analysis.** The brand **precedes the product**: audiences commit to a release
on the name alone, years before seeing it. Cross-media semiotic coherence
(emblem, merchandise, communication, in-game design) is high and single-handed.
The canon is not maintained as a museum because most of it is not owned.

**Signals.** *Strong*: purchase on trust; a proprietary vocabulary adopted by
press and public. *Weak*: brand equity concentrated in one surname and one
drawing hand — two individuals.

## MODULE 4 — Product and catalogue

- **Output**: extremely low volume. Two shipped titles in roughly a decade, plus
  extended editions and ports `to-source` (tier C).
- **Frontlist/backlist ratio**: effectively all frontlist. There is no backlist
  annuity, because the pre-2015 catalogue belongs elsewhere.
- **Formats**: console-first, timed platform exclusivity, later PC and expanded
  editions; collector editions and a merchandise line around the emblem.
- **Curation**: one person decides. No published criteria.
- **Sub-brands**: a declared media division for film/TV/music `to-source`.

**Analysis.** The catalogue is a **flow, not an asset** — the defining economic
weakness of this house. Each title must carry its own cost and the interval
before the next. Limited editions create real scarcity in the merchandise line;
in games, scarcity is not a lever.

**Signals.** *Strong*: each release treated as an event in itself. *Weak*: **no
backlist to finance frontlist risk** — the studio's most serious structural
exposure, and the one that most distinguishes it from the label archetypes this
framework was designed for.

## MODULE 5 — Economic model

| Item | Estimate | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Headcount | low hundreds | D — `to-source` |
| Revenue | not disclosed | D — `to-source` |
| Profitability | not disclosed | D — `to-source` |
| IP ownership of the first title | reported as retained by the studio | C — contested, `to-source` |

**Revenue streams**: development funding from publishing partners; royalties;
merchandising; licensing for adaptations; a declared media division.

**Relationship to outside capital**: no publicly reported outside shareholding
or acquisition `to-source`. Independence appears to be financed **project by
project** through publisher agreements rather than by accumulated capital.

**Analysis.** The stream that pays the bills and the stream that carries meaning
are **the same**: the game. That is unusual and, contrary to intuition, a
weakness — there is no ballast. The model would not survive the loss of publisher
appetite for large-budget authored work, which is precisely the appetite that
2015 showed can vanish.

Retained IP ownership, if confirmed, would be the decisive fact of the whole
case: it converts a work-for-hire studio into an estate builder, and is the only
visible path toward the backlist that Module 4 finds missing.

**Signals.** *Strong*: revenue diversification (adaptations, merchandise, media)
**without diversification of the proposition**. *Weak*: independence sustained by
partner willingness rather than by owned reserves — a choice that is real, but
renewed at every contract.

## MODULE 6 — Production and logistics

- **Engine and tooling**: the first title was built on an engine licensed from
  another studio, subsequently developed in-house `to-source` (tier C). Adopting
  external technology to ship the first product is a decisive early call: it
  bought years.
- **Location**: single Tokyo studio; a foreign branch was opened and later
  restructured `to-source` (tier D, poorly documented).
- **Cycle length**: four to six years per title.
- **Quality standards**: technical presentation and performance capture of film
  actors are the publicly claimed differentiators.

**Analysis.** Fabrication quality is a **public argument**, not a hidden cost —
the studio communicates about its technology and its casting as part of the work.
Strategic partners: the engine provider (initially), the publishing platform, and
the performance-capture pipeline. The actors themselves are closer to strategic
than to commodity: their faces are part of the product.

**Signals.** *Strong*: named partners, publicly credited and repeatedly used.
*Weak*: dependence on external engine technology at the founding moment — solved,
but at the cost of a multi-year internal tooling effort.

## MODULE 7 — Distribution

- **Channels**: console platform holders; digital storefronts; PC distribution
  through a third-party publisher; direct-to-consumer for merchandise only.
- **D2C share**: negligible for games, meaningful for merchandise `to-source`.
- **Geography**: global release; Japanese studio with a Western-facing cast and
  setting — a deliberate export posture from the first title onward.
- **Scarcity doctrine**: none for games (abundance); real scarcity in the
  merchandise line.

**Analysis.** The house **does not own the customer relationship** for its main
product. Platform holders and storefronts do. What the house owns is the
**attention relationship** — direct, through the founder's personal channels.
This split is the key asymmetry of the case: strong audience ownership, zero
transaction ownership.

**Signals.** *Strong*: merchandise D2C as the one owned commercial channel.
*Weak*: total intermediation of the core product; timed exclusivity trades reach
for financing.

## MODULE 8 — Marketing and communication

- **Channels**: the founder's personal social accounts; industry showcase events;
  trailers treated as authored short films; a podcast; published books
  `to-source`.
- **Register**: personal, continuous, and largely non-commercial — daily posts on
  films, books and music unrelated to any product.
- **Rhythm**: constant low-intensity presence between releases, punctuated by
  showcase-event reveals.
- **Prescribers**: film directors, actors and musicians act as validators, which
  imports credibility from outside the games industry.

**Analysis.** The communication **maintains a world**, it does not sell products.
Between releases there is nothing to sell, and the channel stays alive on
cultural recommendation alone — this is the mechanism that keeps a four-to-six
year interval survivable. The claimed anti-marketing posture is **partly
verifiable**: trailers are unusually authorial and uninformative by design, but
platform-showcase placement is conventional and heavily negotiated.

**Signals.** *Strong*: owned channel outperforming rented ones; editorial objects
(podcast, books, exhibitions) doing brand work over time. *Weak*: the owned
channel is a **personal account**, not an institutional one — it is not
transferable.

## MODULE 9 — Culture, community, governance

- **Relationship to creators**: a stable core team carried across the 2015
  rupture — several long-standing collaborators followed the founder into the new
  company `to-source`. This is the strongest available evidence that loyalty here
  is personal and durable.
- **Non-founding key figures**: the art director above all; also long-serving
  producers and technical leads.
- **Public litigation**: none reported for entity (II) `to-source`. The 2015
  events concerned the former employer.
- **Community**: unusually literate and cross-disciplinary; overlaps with film
  and music audiences rather than being confined to games.
- **Succession**: **no public succession plan.**

**Analysis.** The house **makes careers** in the sense that its alumni carry
recognisable authorship. But its critical functions are **personal, not
institutionalised**: direction, writing, design and art direction rest on two
named individuals with no documented redundancy, no written method, and no
declared successor.

This is the decisive finding of the study. Everything that makes the house
coherent is the same thing that makes it mortal.

**Signals.** *Strong*: collaborators who followed the founder through a corporate
rupture — the highest possible loyalty signal. *Weak*: single points of failure
on both the authorial and the visual function; no evidence of codification.

## MODULE 10 — Trajectory and inflection points

1. **2015 — the rupture.** Loss of employer, team, catalogue and an in-progress
   project. What saved the house: a platform holder's willingness to finance an
   unproven original concept, and the founder's personal reputation.
2. **2019 — first release.** Commercially adequate, critically divisive; the
   invented genre was neither an obvious success nor a failure `to-source`.
3. **2021-2023 — consolidation.** Extended editions, PC distribution, a declared
   media division, and announced projects with **multiple different publishers**
   — a deliberate exit from single-partner dependence.
4. **2025 — second release.** Sequel to the original IP.

**Analysis.** The crises **hardened the thesis** rather than negotiating it: the
post-2015 output is more idiosyncratic, not less. There is no visible instance of
commercial success deforming the curation — chiefly because there has been no
runaway commercial success to deform it.

The current position is **expansion under founder dependency**: more projects,
more partners, more media, all still routed through one person.

**Signals.** *Strong*: survival of a total institutional collapse with the
proposition intact; multi-partner strategy as deliberate de-risking. *Weak*:
expansion of surface area without expansion of the decision-making base.

## FINAL BLOCK — Transposition 🔍 (suggested)

The only block permitted to address *Dans l'Absolu*. Suggested, not adopted.

### Three transposable principles

1. **Own the attention relationship even when you cannot own the transaction**
   (Module 7). A house whose product passes through intermediaries can still hold
   the audience directly. For a label: the owned channel is the asset that
   survives any distributor.
2. **Build the catalogue as an estate, not as a flow** (Modules 4 & 5). This
   case's central weakness is instructive precisely because the house cannot fix
   it: it does not own its own history. A label that retains its masters holds
   what this studio lost.
3. **Let the making be visible in the made thing** (Module 3). Consistency of hand outperforms
   consistency of charter — provided the hand is present.

### Two guard-rails

1. **Codify what only one person knows, or accept that the house is mortal**
   (Module 9). If the method is never written, there is nothing to transmit. This
   is a structural question and must be answered structurally, not by trust.
2. **Do not build the owned channel on a personal account** (Module 8). An
   audience gathered under a private name cannot be handed on. Separate the
   founder's voice from the house's channel early, while it costs nothing.

### One concrete short-term experiment

Write down, for a single completed work, the decisions that were actually made
and the reasons given at the time — a production log kept during the work rather
than reconstructed after it. One work, one document. It tests whether the house's
method is expressible at all, which is the question Module 9 raises and which
no amount of intention answers.

# Case Study — Stones Throw Records

*Applied instance of [[framework-etude-de-cas]]. Facts, analysis and
transposition are kept in separate registers throughout.*

---

## MODULE 1 — Identity & genesis

**Facts (B/C)**
- Independent record label, Los Angeles (Highland Park HQ), founded 1996 by
  Chris Manak (Peanut Butter Wolf), a DJ/producer from San Jose with a business
  marketing degree. Offices in LA and London (source C, label info pages).
- Founding wound: Manak's partner, rapper Charizma, was killed in 1993 at age
  20. The label's first release (1996) was their posthumous single "My World
  Premiere" — the label exists first to publish a dead friend's work.
- The name comes from a family saying the duo used to joke about — private,
  affective origin rather than market-facing branding.
- Prior experience: Manak had co-released a record in college on a micro-label
  (500 copies, $500 from his father, unmastered, cut-and-paste artwork) —
  a full dress rehearsal in DIY economics before founding.
- Market context: mid-90s West Coast dominated by commercial gangsta rap;
  Stones Throw occupied the sample-heavy underground left vacant.

**Analysis**
Foundation by interior necessity, not opportunity. The grief-origin gives the
label a non-negotiable core: publishing what deserves to exist precedes
publishing what sells. This is the root of thirty years of coherence.

**Signals**: strong — founder had already failed cheaply once (PMR Records)
before founding; the origin story is public, stable, and retold at every
anniversary without embellishment.

---

## MODULE 2 — Thesis & positioning

**Facts (B)**
- Manak's stated doctrine (Huck interview, paraphrased): he signs what sounds
  as good as the best records in his ~100,000-piece personal collection; the
  only common trait across the roster is mutual belief between artist and
  founder. As his taste broadened, so did the output.
- Trained in marketing, he describes having done roughly the opposite of what
  he was taught (B, direct interview).
- Genre span: underground hip-hop → funk/soul revival → psychedelic rock,
  synth-pop, city pop, electronic. First non-hip-hop release as early as 1999.
- LA Weekly (20th anniversary): described the label as a perpetually evolving
  experiment (C).

**Analysis**
The unity of the catalog is neither genre nor era: it is one person's ear,
institutionalized. Differentiation is structural — a curation model where the
filter (the founder's collection-calibrated taste) cannot be copied, only
imitated. The refused space: chart logic, A&R by data, genre lock-in.

**Signals**: strong — the public buys "a Stones Throw record" across genres,
which is the definition of the brand preceding the product (see Module 3).

---

## MODULE 3 — Art direction & brand

**Facts (B/C)**
- Long-tenured internal art direction (Jeff Jank, credited across the classic
  era) shaped a recognizable visual world: character-driven covers (Quasimoto),
  conceptual packaging (heart-shaped vinyl for Mayer Hawthorne — which Manak
  initially resisted as gimmicky, then accepted; the public loved it).
- Canon management is a core activity: 20th-anniversary Madvillainy re-cut by
  Bernie Grundman at 45rpm; Donuts 20th-anniversary Audiophile Edition
  (lacquer cut at Bernie Grundman, pressed at Fidelity Record Pressing,
  180g 2LP at 45rpm, Stoughton jackets), store-exclusive.
- 2014 feature documentary *Our Vinyl Weighs a Ton* (dir. Jeff Broadway), with
  testimonials from Kanye West, Tyler, the Creator, Questlove — the label's
  history itself became a branded editorial object.
- New imprint "Listening Position" launched for audiophile-grade reissues
  (first release: Philip Cohran repress) (A, label site).

**Analysis**
The brand is a trust contract: the logo functions as a quality seal strong
enough that catalog, anniversaries and even a cocktail bar (Module 8) extend
it without dilution. Canon is curated like a museum with a living wing —
reissues are treated as first-class editorial events, not inventory recycling.

**Signals**: strong — third parties (retailers like Turntable Lab) describe
the label itself, not just its artists, as the reason to buy (C).

## MODULE 4 — Product & catalog

**Facts (A/B/C)**
- Formats: vinyl-first identity; majority of releases pressed on vinyl
  alongside digital (C). Active 45s program explicitly framed by Manak as
  preservation of an endangered format, accepting runs of ~1,000 units per
  title (B, label's own history page).
- Limited editions: colored vinyl, anniversary audiophile editions, store
  exclusives; 30th-anniversary limited clear vinyl series announced (A).
- Imprints: Now-Again (founded 2002 by then-GM Egon as a reissue subsidiary
  for funk/soul archaeology; incorporated independently 2010) ; Listening
  Position (audiophile reissues, recent) (B/C).
- Backlist depth: Madvillainy (2004) and Donuts (2006) remain flagship sellers
  two decades on; catalog includes RIAA-gold single "Homage" (Mild High Club)
  and double-platinum-trajectory Aloe Blacc's *Good Things* (gold in UK,
  France, Germany, Australia) (C).

**Analysis**
The catalog is an appreciating asset: two canonical albums finance risk-taking
on the frontlist. The imprint logic is laboratory-segmentation — archival work
(Now-Again) and audiophile work (Listening Position) get their own names so
the mothership stays legible as "new music, curated."

**Signals**: strong — backlist-as-endowment model; willingness to run
economically marginal formats (45s) for doctrinal reasons.

## MODULE 5 — Business model

**Facts (A/B/D)**
- Revenue streams: physical (vinyl/45s/CD/cassette), digital/streaming,
  publishing & sync (in-house division), merchandising (clothing, accessories,
  skate decks, artwork), a physical venue (Gold Line bar), licensing.
- Publishing: Stones Throw Publishing, born "out of necessity" to serve
  roster artists without representation, grew into a full worldwide admin +
  sync arm; signed Songtrust for global administration (2021). Roster includes
  Knxwledge, Kiefer, Mndsgn — with co-writes reaching Kendrick Lamar and
  Tyler, the Creator records (B, Music Business Worldwide; GM Jason McGuire
  quoted).
- Revenue estimate: ~$7.2M annual (2026) per RocketReach — **level D,
  low-credibility estimate, unverified, retain as order of magnitude only**.
- Ownership: no public record of outside capital or acquisition found in this
  research pass; treated as founder-controlled independent — `to-source` for
  formal cap-table confirmation.

**Analysis**
The pattern is diversification of revenue without diversification of the
propos: every stream (publishing, merch, bar, reissues) monetizes the same
trust asset built by curation. Publishing is the maturity marker — it converts
taste (signing Knxwledge early) into long-tail rights income when those
writers reach Kendrick/Tyler-scale placements.

**Signals**: strong — sync/publishing built in-house before outsourcing admin;
weak/watch — physical-retail exposure and venue economics are cyclical.

## MODULE 6 — Production & logistics

**Facts (A/B)**
- Named, publicized fabrication partners for premium editions: Bernie Grundman
  (lacquer cutting), Fidelity Record Pressing, Stoughton (jackets) — the
  supply chain itself is marketed (A, release pages).
- Standards on premium line: 180g, 45rpm 2LP cuts, deluxe printed jackets;
  behind-the-scenes manufacturing videos published as content (A).
- Early era: hand-screened covers, few-hundred-copy runs, founder handling
  production and distribution personally (C).

**Analysis**
Fabrication quality migrated from constraint (DIY scarcity) to argument
(audiophile positioning). Naming the pressing plant and cutting engineer
converts logistics into narrative — the chain of custody is part of the
product.

**Signals**: strong — partners are stable, named, and prestige-bearing.

---

## MODULE 7 — Distribution

**Facts (A/C)**
- Direct-to-consumer store (stonesthrow.com) with store-exclusive flagship
  editions (Donuts Audiophile "shipping now exclusively from the Stones Throw
  store"), plus explicit accommodation of the retail network ("international
  record shops will have pre-orders coming soon") (A).
- Wide third-party retail presence (Turntable Lab, Music Direct, etc.) and
  standard digital/streaming distribution (C).
- Geographic reach: LA + London offices; strong international collector
  markets (C). Specific distributor contracts: not established in this pass —
  `to-source`.

**Analysis**
Two-tier doctrine: the most desirable objects anchor the owned channel (D2C
margin + customer relationship), while standard editions feed the record-shop
ecosystem that made the label's reputation. Scarcity is real (limited cuts)
but managed so retailers are partners, not victims.

**Signals**: strong — exclusives structured to pull traffic to owned store
without starving shops.

---

## MODULE 8 — Marketing & communication

**Facts (A/B)**
- Owned channels: site + newsletter ("No junk. Just the good stuff") (A).
- Editorial objects doing brand work at long timescale: the 2014 documentary,
  anniversary campaigns (Donuts 20, label 30), artist mini-docs and
  making-of content (A/C).
- Physical embassy: Gold Line (2018), a cocktail bar under the Highland Park
  HQ, built around a 7,500-record collection and all-vinyl DJ sets (C).
- Founder's stated ambivalence toward marketing orthodoxy: marketing degree,
  inverted in practice (B).

**Analysis**
This is trust-economy communication: rituals (anniversaries), places (the
bar), and artifacts (films, audiophile editions) compound loyalty instead of
buying attention. The "anti-marketing" is verifiable in practice — the label
resisted a gimmick (heart-shaped vinyl) and only conceded to serve the artist,
which itself became the story.

**Signals**: strong — owned-channel primacy; a bar as standing proof that the
brand is a world, not a feed.

## MODULE 9 — Culture, community & governance

**Facts (B/C)**
- Career-making record: Madlib, J Dilla, Quasimoto, Aloe Blacc, Mayer
  Hawthorne, Dam-Funk, Knxwledge, Anderson .Paak (via NxWorries), Sudan
  Archives, Mndsgn — several developed from obscurity to global careers.
- Key non-founder figures: Eothen "Egon" Alapatt, GM 2000–2011, who A&R'd or
  managed most of the classic-era albums (Madvillainy, Donuts, Good Things)
  before leaving to run Now-Again (B).
- Governance cautionary tale (adjacent, post-departure): in 2024 Madlib sued
  Egon alleging self-dealing, opaque accounting and mismanagement across
  jointly-held vehicles (Madicine Show, Rapp Cats); MF DOOM's estate also
  litigated over the producer's notebooks. Claims are allegations, not
  findings — but the structural lesson stands regardless of verdict (B,
  Variety/Mixmag/RA).
- Community: collectors, DJ culture, physical events at Gold Line; alumni
  (Aloe Blacc, Anderson .Paak) remain associated with the label's story.

**Analysis**
The label institutionalized taste (the founder) but for a decade concentrated
operations in one GM — brilliant while aligned, and the post-departure
litigation around that same figure illustrates the systemic risk of
personal-relationship governance without independent accounting, clean entity
separation and transparent books. The lesson is structural, not moral.

**Signals**: strong — artist development track record; weak/warning — critical
functions historically personal rather than institutional; financial
transparency disputes in the ecosystem around ex-management.

## MODULE 10 — Trajectory & inflection points

**Facts (B/C)**
- 1996 founding (SF Bay Area) → 2000 move to Los Angeles (arrival of Egon,
  Jank, Madlib) — the transformation from bedroom label to institution.
- 2004 Madvillainy / 2006 Donuts: canonical peak; Donuts released days before
  J Dilla's death — the label became steward of an estate-level legacy.
- 2006–2010: genre broadening (Aloe Blacc's mainstream success, funk revival
  via Dam-Funk) without abandoning the underground core.
- 2011: Egon departs; label continues, disproving single-operator dependence.
- 2010s–2020s: diversification (publishing arm, Gold Line bar 2018, imprints),
  viral-era signings (NxWorries 2016, Sudan Archives), 30th anniversary
  campaign underway (2026).
- Founder's own retrospective lucidity (B): the record industry as he knew it
  collapsed ("no record stores, nobody buys records"), and he states he might
  not have started the label had he known — yet the label outlived the
  collapse by rebuilding on vinyl, D2C, publishing and brand.

**Analysis**
Every inflection consolidated the thesis instead of renegotiating it: the LA
move professionalized curation; Dilla's death made the label a custodian
(deepening, not exploiting, the canon); the industry collapse was survived by
monetizing trust (D2C, editions, publishing) rather than chasing formats.
Longevity: 30 years, exceptional for an independent in this sector.

## BLOC FINAL — Transposition 🔍 (suggéré — décision humaine requise, Cmd 13)

**Trois principes transposables**
1. *(M2/M3)* Institutionalize one ear: the catalog's unity is a single
   calibrated taste made public and consistent — Dans l'Absolu's equivalent is
   the trait-table/curation discipline already in place for Album 01; the
   framework confirms it as the core asset to protect.
2. *(M6/M7)* Name the chain, own the flagship: publicize fabrication partners
   (cutting, pressing) as part of the object's identity, and reserve the most
   desirable edition for the owned channel — compatible with the gift-economy
   doctrine (the 300-copy curated-shop gift IS the owned-channel gesture).
3. *(M8)* Build slow editorial objects: documentary, anniversary rituals,
   a physical place — Dans l'Absolu's site-as-journal and the *Suffering &
   Surrender* retrospective already follow this grammar; treat them as the
   marketing, not as accessories to it.

**Deux garde-fous**
1. *(M9)* No critical function without books: any future collaborator handling
   money or rights operates under transparent accounting and clean entity
   separation from day one — the Egon-era litigation shows the cost of
   relationship-based governance. Maps to the existing human-gate and
   Gardien-du-Protocole design.
2. *(M5)* Revenue diversification must never diversify the propos: each new
   stream must monetize the same trust asset, or be refused.

**Une expérimentation concrète**
A "chain-of-custody" block on each release fiche (and later on the site):
tape machine, cutting, pressing, printing partners named — turning Sidy's
analog finality (Revox → vinyl) into narrative, Stones Throw-style.

dédiées, jamais supposées (Cmd 3) ; adoption de tout principe ci-dessus =
